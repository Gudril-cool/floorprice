FloorPrice

The conservative bound. Every protocol below is priced like its category’s laggards — the value a farmer can underwrite. Anything above the floor is upside, never the plan.

How the floor is underwritten

For each protocol that records no token, take its category’s tokenized peers — protocols with a market cap and positive TVL — and their market-cap-to-TVL ratios. The snapshot carries no FDV field, so market cap stands in for FDV and the page says so. Ratios are winsorized into the global 10th–90th percentile band, then the floor multiple is the category’s 25th percentile, shrunk toward the global 25th percentile with a 6-peer weight. A category with one peer barely moves the global floor; a category with fifteen peers mostly speaks for itself. The multiple is clamped to 0.01x–1.50x.

Implied floor value = TVL × floor multiple × survival × breadth. Survival is age only: under 90 days 0.75, 90–364 days 0.85, 1–3 years 0.95, over 3 years 1.00, unknown listing date 0.85. A small discount for very young protocols lives inside that factor. Breadth is chain count: 1 chain 0.92, 2 chains 0.97, 3 or more 1.00. There is deliberately no momentum term: a floor that rises with hype is not a floor. The seven-day change is shown for context and never enters the price.

The board also carries each row’s median-implied value — the same formula at the category’s shrunk median multiple — so the reader can see floor against typical at a glance. The floor is a pricing bound, not a promise: a protocol on this board may still have a token its record does not carry, and products of live tokens (wrapped, staked and bridged variants, bridge and exchange categories, exchange-prefixed names) are set aside, never ranked.
